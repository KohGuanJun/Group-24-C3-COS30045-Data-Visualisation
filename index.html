Glossary & Data Notes

Data Source

BITRE – Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics. Hospitalised Injury Data (2011–2021), Australian Road Traffic dataset. All figures represent cases where a road-crash victim was admitted to hospital (not merely treated at an emergency department and discharged).

Key Metric Definitions

Hospitalised Injuries: The total number of people admitted to hospital as a result of a road traffic crash injury in the reference period.

Total Bed Days: The cumulative number of nights patients spent in hospital across all admissions. A higher figure indicates greater overall burden on the health system.

Average Bed Days (per admission): Total Bed Days divided by the number of Hospitalised Injuries. This serves as a proxy for injury severity — a higher average indicates patients required longer hospital recovery.

Demographic & Geographic Breakdowns

Road User Type: The role of the injured person in the crash (e.g., Car driver, Motorcyclist, Pedal cyclist, Pedestrian) based on official BITRE classifications.

ABS Remoteness Area: Geographic classification from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ASGS Remoteness Structure) — Major Cities, Inner/Outer Regional, Remote, and Very Remote — reflecting access to services.

Age Group & Sex: Grouped age brackets (0–7, 8–16, 17–25, 26–39, 40–64, 65–74, 75+) and recorded sex (Female, Male, Intersex/indeterminate/missing).

First Nations Status: Compares hospitalised crash injuries among Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander (First Nations) peoples and Non-Indigenous Australians across age, road user group, and remoteness.

Important Data Limitations

Population size differences: Direct comparison of raw injury counts across jurisdictions (e.g., NSW vs. Northern Territory) is misleading without accounting for population size — NSW has ~32× the population of the NT (~16× its total hospitalisations). Use the % Change Since 2011 and Average Bed Days views to compare relative growth and severity fairly.

Year-on-year variation (COVID-19): The 2020 figures reflect COVID-19 travel restrictions, which reduced road traffic volumes and hospitalisations nationwide.

Unallocated, missing & suppressed records: Around 7,591 national traffic records lack an allocated state (explaining the 410,884 national demographic total vs. 403,293 state total). In the First Nations dataset, 66 small-count cells marked n.p. (not published for privacy) were filtered out during KNIME data cleaning.

Methodology Notes

Weighted average bed days: National and multi-year average bed days are computed as a weighted average (Total Bed Days ÷ Total Hospitalised Injuries = 4.89 days) rather than an unweighted mean of annual averages.

% Change Since 2011 (indexed mode): Each state’s value is expressed relative to its own 2011 baseline: ((value − baseline) ÷ baseline) × 100, removing absolute population size bias.
